          My work moves between the natural and the artificial. I am interested in the ways materials and objects absorb, retain, and transform information. Through mold-making, casting, sculpting, and experimental processes, I think about how things are preserved, how they break down, and what they become.

          I am drawn to the instability of replication. Casting promises a one-to-one reproduction, but something is always lost or changed in the process: an air pocket forms and something new emerges, something previously inaccessible. In these moments, a material departs from its original identity and becomes something unfamiliar. The attempt to preserve something becomes an act of transformation.

          I am interested in making things that are 90% of this planet and 10% augmented. I am curious about what happens when natural processes are artificially reproduced, when manufactured objects begin to resemble geological artifacts, and when nature absorbs the structures built to contain it.

          Bio

          Blossom Bogen-Froese is an artist living and working in the Hudson Valley, New York.
